Local only

It runs entirely on your Mac. No account, no network access, no telemetry.

Preview first

Every cleanup shows each item, its size and the total it frees before anything is deleted.

Your say-so

Nothing runs without it. Automations in Auto mode are limited to regenerable items.

The problem

“System Data: 180 GB.” And no way to look inside.

If you work with Node, Rust, Python, Xcode, Docker or local models, that number keeps growing and macOS keeps quiet about why.

It can’t see hidden folders

~/.npm, ~/Library/Caches and ~/.cargo never show up in macOS Storage.

It lumps everything together

Caches, toolchains, simulators and model files all end up under “System Data” or “Developer”.

It never says what is safe to delete

You get a size, not an answer to “can I remove this?”. Other cleaners are paid, closed, and scan the whole machine. strg is free and looks at your home folder, where developer tools leave their caches.

How it works

Scan, review, clean. In that order.

  1. 01

    Scan

    strg walks your home folder and groups what it finds by meaning: node_modules, Xcode, Docker, AI models. Each group shows the space you would actually get back.

  2. 02

    Review

    You get a plan: every item with its size, and a plain total such as “frees ≈ 868 MB”. Tick what you want, then choose the Trash or permanent deletion.

  3. 03

    Clean

    Only after you confirm. Every action is written to an activity log file.

Safe by default

It deletes files, so it shows you first.

That is the trade-off, and strg is built around it. Every finding carries a badge and a plain-language note on what it is and how it comes back.

Regenerable

Comes back automatically when needed. The only kind Auto automations may touch.

Caution

Can be recreated, but it takes time or a download. Shown for you to review, never cleaned automatically.

Personal

Your data. strg lists it, and you review it yourself.

Leaves work in progress alone

Projects with uncommitted git changes and projects with a running dev server are skipped.

Skips anything in use

If a process has it open, strg tells you which one and leaves it where it is.

Refuses risky paths

Symlinked and protected folders are refused outright.

Counts the real size

The total is what you will actually free, including hard links (pnpm) and sparse files (Docker disk images).

Trash or permanent, your call

Permanent deletion cannot be undone, which is why the plan always comes first.

A log of every action

Everything strg does is written to a file you can read.

Features

Look around. Then let it keep watch.

Explorer

A treemap and a sortable list of everything in your home folder, hidden folders included. Show any item in Finder or move it to the Trash.

Fast to come back to

strg keeps a saved index and follows filesystem events, so rescans take seconds.

Menu bar agent

Free space sits next to the icon. When it drops below a limit you set, strg sends a low-space alert with a one-click review.

Automations

Rules that run on a schedule or when space runs low, in one of three modes: Notify, Ask or Auto. Auto is allowed only for regenerable items. You can also limit a rule to items that have sat idle for a set number of days.

A command line, too

A strg command-line tool ships alongside the app.

$ strg scan
$ strg suggest
$ strg clean --dry-run # preview only, deletes nothing

A catalog of about 60 rules

Each rule knows what a folder is and how it comes back. They cover the usual suspects:

First launch

strg is ad-hoc signed, not notarized by Apple, so macOS will warn you the first time you open it. To open it anyway, go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll to Security and choose Open Anyway. On macOS 13 and 14 you can also Control-click the app and choose Open.

Before you clean

strg frees disk space by deleting files such as caches and build folders. It shows you exactly what will go before each cleanup, and deleting is permanent unless you choose the Trash. Provided as is, without warranty; use at your own risk.

strg is free.

If it gave you back some disk space and you would like to chip in, you can. There is no catch and nothing is locked behind it.
